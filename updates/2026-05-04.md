# [Week of 2026-05-04] — Why I bought a Mac Mini and set it up as an AI development machine

---

Reading about AI isn't the same as building with it.

I've spent 20+ years in software engineering and data engineering. I've led teams, architected systems, reviewed papers, attended conferences. But somewhere along the way, I noticed something: the gap between understanding AI conceptually and actually deploying it is enormous — and the only way to close that gap is to build.

So I did.

A few months ago, I had a conversation with a couple of small business owners — a contractor, a local service provider. Smart, hardworking people. They told me the same thing independently: social media presence makes or breaks their business. But managing it consistently? That's a part-time job they don't have time for.

That conversation stuck with me. I decided to build something that solves that problem. Not a prototype. Not a proof of concept. A real tool, for real people, running on real infrastructure.

That journey starts with a $600 Mac Mini sitting on my desk. I set it up as a dedicated AI development machine — configured for remote access, running a local AI agent framework called OpenClaw, wired up to handle real workloads. I documented every step so any engineer can replicate it from scratch.

This is the first post in a series where I build in public — the environment, the platform, the product, and every hard lesson in between.

If you're an engineer who wants to stay sharp with AI by actually building things, follow along.
